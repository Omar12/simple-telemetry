# Product

## Register

Micro-Telemetry

## Users

Independent bloggers and small publishers who want simple, private traffic insight without third-party analytics. They are typically checking performance after publishing, reviewing which posts are getting attention, and looking for a fast read on traffic patterns without learning a complicated analytics suite.

## Product Purpose

This product provides lightweight first-party blog analytics with a private dashboard. It exists to answer the essential publishing questions, page views, unique visitors, top pages, referrers, trends, and device/browser mix, while staying simple, privacy-conscious, and easy to trust. Success means a publisher can understand what is being read and where traffic is coming from in a few moments, without surveillance-heavy analytics or operational complexity.

## Brand Personality

Modern, clean, focused. The interface should feel current and credible, but never loud or trendy for its own sake. It should communicate restraint, clarity, and trust, especially around privacy and data handling.

## Anti-references

Avoid the complexity and cognitive overhead of GA4. Avoid noisy growth-hack dashboards packed with competing charts, callouts, and vanity metrics. Avoid neon crypto aesthetics, exaggerated dark-mode theatrics, and interfaces that look speculative rather than dependable.

## Design Principles

- Prioritize signal over volume: show the few metrics a small publisher actually needs, not every metric that could exist.
- Make privacy legible: the interface should visibly reinforce that analytics are lightweight, first-party, and anonymous.
- Reduce interpretation cost: users should understand what happened and what matters at a glance.
- Keep the product operationally modest: simple implementation choices should still look intentional and polished.
- Treat familiarity as a feature: standard dashboard patterns are preferable when they make the tool easier to trust and faster to use.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA contrast and interaction clarity. Respect reduced-motion preferences and avoid decorative motion that distracts from the task. Maintain strong visual contrast, clear labeling, and predictable structure so the dashboard remains usable across a wide range of users and viewing conditions.
